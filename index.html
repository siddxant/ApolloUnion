Flagship Research Program

Detecting disease years before it appears

Standard risk models were built on Western populations. South Asians are systematically misclassified by them, and the clinical consequences are significant.

South Asians develop type 2 diabetes at lower body weight, cardiac disease nearly a decade earlier than European counterparts, and chronic kidney disease with almost no prior warning. The Framingham and QRISK models most clinicians rely on were not calibrated for this population.

Apollo Union builds early-detection models using longitudinal laboratory data that captures physiological change over years, not a single cross-sectional reading. The result is a picture of where a patient is going, not only where they stand today.

Six further lines of inquiry

Diagnostics

Indian Reference Intervals

Most laboratory normal ranges were derived from Western cohorts. A misaligned reference interval undermines every diagnostic decision made with it.

Deriving India-specific reference intervals from millions of laboratory results is a genuine scientific contribution that improves accuracy across every test type. It carries low regulatory risk, produces publishable results in months rather than years, and builds the data pipelines and institutional trust the larger programs require.

References

PubMed: Indian population laboratory reference intervals IFCC Committee on Reference Intervals (C-RI)
Near-term / Publishable

Pharmaceuticals

Real-World Evidence

Clinical trials rarely show how a drug performs in Indian patients. Apollo Union generates population-specific effectiveness and safety data from actual prescribing records.

Indian patients may respond differently to drugs due to genetics, diet, and comorbidity patterns that are not represented in trial populations. Real-world evidence from prescription and outcomes data gives pharmaceutical companies the population-specific evidence their regulatory submissions and commercial decisions require, and is likely the largest long-term revenue line in the portfolio.

References

CDSCO: Guidance Documents for Pharmaceuticals PubMed: Real-world evidence and pharmacovigilance in India
Long-term / Core revenue

Global Health

Antimicrobial Resistance Mapping

India carries a disproportionate share of the global burden of drug-resistant infections. Combining culture results with prescribing data at scale reveals where resistance is emerging.

Culture test results, antibiotic prescription records, and pharmacy sales data, combined at scale, can identify which prescribing patterns are driving resistance and where outbreaks are forming before they spread. This is research fundable by global health bodies, publishable in the journals that shape international policy, and directly actionable by regulators and public health agencies.

References

WHO GLASS: Global Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance ICMR Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance Network
High-profile / Fundable

Clinical Operations

Trial Recruitment

Finding eligible patients for a pharmaceutical trial typically takes months of chart review and site coordination. Structured clinical data makes the same search possible in minutes.

Pharmaceutical companies pay significantly for patient identification, and the current state of manual recruitment is expensive enough that even a partial solution carries clear commercial value. This is a near-term product alongside the longer research programs, with a straightforward value proposition that requires no new data collection and no patient-facing infrastructure.

References

Clinical Trials Registry India (CTRI) PubMed: Clinical trial recruitment challenges in India
Commercial / Near-term

Supply Chain

Pharmacy Demand Forecasting

Regional medicine supply is poorly matched to demand, producing stockouts in some areas and waste in others. Laboratory and prescription trends make district-level forecasting tractable.

Seasonal disease patterns, diagnostic trends, and prescription data, combined across a large pharmacy network, allow demand forecasting at a granularity that no buyer currently has access to. This is a practical product with clear commercial buyers in pharmaceutical distribution and hospital procurement, and accuracy improves as the underlying data grows.

References

National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) PubMed: Medicine supply chain and demand in India
Operational / Commercial

Patient Safety

Prescribing Quality

Dangerous drug interactions, inappropriate antibiotics, and polypharmacy in elderly patients represent a largely invisible clinical burden. Flagging these at the point of prescription requires no new data collection.

This addresses a problem that regulators, hospitals, and insurers all have strong incentive to solve, which makes it easier to deploy than most clinical AI applications. It is clinically meaningful from day one, and the signal improves as prescription volume grows. The same data layer supports antibiotic stewardship programs, which are a growing regulatory focus across India.

References

WHO: Rational Use of Medicines Programme PubMed: Polypharmacy and prescribing quality in India
Deployable / Clinical

The Data Foundation

Every product begins here

Each research program and commercial product rests on the same foundation: a structured, longitudinal view of Indian clinical data at a scale no other organisation has assembled.

The data already exists. It sits across thousands of diagnostic laboratories, pharmacies, and hospital systems, collected over years of routine clinical care. Apollo Union makes it legible, structured, and analytically useful for the first time at this scale.

What this enables is not generic machine learning applied to health data. It is population-specific medical knowledge, derived from the actual clinical experience of Indian patients.
