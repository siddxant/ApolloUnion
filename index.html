Three problems we are solving

01
Flagship

Silent diseases that medicine misses in Indians

Western risk models are miscalibrated for South Asians. Indians develop diabetes at lower BMI, heart disease a decade earlier, and CKD largely undetected. Apollo's longitudinal lab data predicts these conditions years before diagnosis, from tests people already get.

CKD is the sharpest wedge. Creatinine and eGFR trends in longitudinal data show kidney function declining years before any clinical symptom. India carries an enormous CKD burden, most undetected, most presenting in late stages when intervention changes little.

The same modelling framework extends to type 2 diabetes onset at sub-diagnostic HbA1c levels, and fatty liver disease identified years before elevated enzymes trigger a clinical referral. Results are publishable in high-impact journals and deployable across Apollo's hospital network from the first release.

02
Pharma Research

Drugs approved on Western trials, prescribed to 140 crore Indians

Pivotal clinical trials rarely include Indian patients. Drug effectiveness and safety signals in Indian populations remain largely unknown until a medicine has already been prescribed at scale. Real-world evidence from 1.1 Crore patient records closes that gap.

Indian patients differ from trial populations in genetics, diet, comorbidity patterns, and drug metabolism. Yet pharmaceutical companies enter the Indian market using trial data from cohorts that do not represent Indian patients.

Apollo Union generates population-specific evidence from actual prescribing and outcome records: which drugs perform differently in Indian patients, where safety signals emerge, and what dosing patterns produce the best results. This is the data pharmaceutical companies need for CDSCO submissions and commercial decisions in the world's most populous drug market.

03
Pharmacogenomics

Drugs that don't work on South Asians, or cause harm

Clopidogrel is among the most widely prescribed medicines for stroke and heart attack prevention. For roughly 1 in 3 Indians carrying CYP2C19 loss-of-function variants, it provides no antiplatelet protection. In some cases, it has caused death. The problem is not the drug. It is that the drug was never studied in this population.

30%
South Asians with CYP2C19 loss-of-function variants
42Crore
Indians with inadequate clopidogrel response

References

PubMed 39009080: Clopidogrel pharmacogenomics and outcomes in South Asian patients PMC6472234: CYP2C19 variants and antiplatelet therapy in South Asians PubMed: Pharmacogenomics in the South Asian population

Clopidogrel is a prodrug activated by CYP2C19. Loss-of-function alleles (*2, *3) are carried by approximately 25 to 30% of South Asians, a significantly higher rate than European populations. In patients with these variants, clopidogrel produces inadequate platelet inhibition, making prescribed therapy effectively useless while the patient and physician believe they are protected.

This is one documented case among many. South Asian populations have distinct pharmacogenomic profiles affecting drug metabolism, receptor sensitivity, and adverse reaction rates across multiple drug classes. Apollo Union's prescription and outcomes data can surface these signals across the Indian population and provide the evidence base for updated prescribing guidelines.
